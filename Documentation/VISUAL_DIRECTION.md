# Visual Direction — JANTAR MANTAR RUN

## Visual target
Aim for a believable, polished 3D game scene rather than a cartoon illustration. Use physically based materials, natural daylight, atmospheric depth, realistic road wear and readable silhouettes. Keep the scene optimized for mobile.

## Core scene composition
- Third-person camera behind and slightly above the runner.
- Three clearly readable lanes with a road vanishing point.
- Delhi-inspired street architecture and signage; use a Jantar Mantar-inspired silhouette as a landmark, not as a claim that every scene is an exact historical reconstruction.
- Indian city traffic, including a red/green city bus, cars, scooters and auto-rickshaws as obstacle variants.
- Sidewalks, barriers, cones, road markings, shopfronts, trees and overhead wires to establish place.
- Warm daylight, restrained color grading, contact shadows and moderate motion blur only where it helps speed readability.

## Character direction
- Main runner: original fictional young adult character, casual streetwear, backpack and clear running silhouette.
- Chasing officers: fictional, non-threatening game characters in recognizable police-inspired uniforms; avoid using a real person's likeness or a real agency insignia without permission.
- Capture beat: brief, non-graphic and comedic. No injury detail.

## Gameplay readability
- Coins: bright gold with a clear rotating highlight.
- Obstacles: distinct silhouettes and lane placement, visible early enough to react.
- Jump/slide: readable pose and timing; avoid visual effects that hide the road.
- HUD: score/distance, coin count, pause and restart; large touch targets for phones.
- Game over: clear result and restart action.

## Asset pipeline
1. Generate or obtain each asset separately (runner, officer, bus, car, scooter, barrier, coin, street props).
2. Prefer GLB/FBX with documented commercial-use rights; keep original source files.
3. Import to Unity; check units, forward axis, pivots, rig, materials, textures and colliders.
4. Create LODs and simplified colliders; compress textures and test mobile frame rate.
5. Keep a record of source, license, creator/tool and import changes for every external asset.
